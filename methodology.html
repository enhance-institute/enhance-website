Methodological Architecture

Analytical Frameworks

Each framework constitutes a distinct analytical layer within ENHANCE's structural audit process. Frameworks are applied deterministically: AI systems extract binary evidence; scoring rules compute outputs. All physical validity judgments remain with the human analyst.

AI extracts binary evidence. Deterministic rules compute scores. Physical validity judgments belong exclusively to the human analyst. This eliminates AI positive bias from the structural audit process.

Analytical Frameworks

Core Diagnostic Layers

Six frameworks constitute the primary structural audit architecture. Each addresses a distinct analytical question. Together they form an integrated diagnostic system for climate-finance structural evaluation.

RFRC™
Regime-Finance-Risk Classification.

Assesses country-finance suitability by classifying structural regime, fiscal position, and risk exposure. Determines whether the proposed financing instrument is structurally appropriate for the recipient country.
ESF™
Environmental Structural Framework.

191-country structural regime classification across three data tiers. Provides the country-level structural baseline against which project design and financing assumptions are evaluated.
C-FAIR™
Climate Finance Alignment and Integrity Rating.

Examines instrument and resilience alignment across hazard exposure, adaptive capacity, and macro-financial fragility. Produces a country-risk grade and financing-structure indication.
CFAM™
Climate Finance Activation Model.

Private finance activation assessment. Evaluates whether private capital mobilization assumptions in project design reflect real activation, risk transfer, or accounting attribution.
ToC:f(x)™
Theory of Change Functional Assessment.

Causal integrity assessment. Examines whether the claimed intervention chain — inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes, impact — is structurally complete and defensible under local physical, economic, and social conditions.
VERA-TCV™
Verification, Evidence, Reasoning, and Attribution — Triangulated Cross-Validation.

Integrated structural diagnosis triangulating four independent models. Applied to 15 GCF projects: 92% directional consistency across 313 assessable signal-year cells confirmed against ex-post performance.
Supplementary Frameworks

Extended Analytical Tools

VORTA™
Value of Outcomes and Risk Transfer Assessment.

Decomposes project value across mitigation, adaptation, social contribution, and ecosystem integrity. Produces a value score, IRRc, and grant requirement signal — expressed in financial terms.
ETP™ v2.1
ENHANCE Project Typology Protocol.

Classifies climate projects across thematic, geographic, and structural dimensions. Enables consistent cross-portfolio comparison and typology-specific audit calibration.
WEP
War Emission Protocol · Scope-W.

Analytical framework for accounting war-related emissions and reconstruction carbon costs within climate finance systems. SSRN Abstract ID: 6969559.

Determination Framework

How Findings Are Expressed

ENHANCE structural findings are expressed using a four-tier determination framework. No finding attributes intentional misconduct. Structure tells the truth — intent is not the object of examination.

Supported
  • Available evidence is consistent with the stated structural claim.
  • The causal mechanism, financial alignment, or outcome pathway can be verified from disclosed documentation.
Conditional
  • Evidence partially supports the claim, subject to specified conditions or assumptions.
  • The finding is valid within defined boundaries; evidence gaps are documented.
Unverified
  • Disclosed evidence is insufficient to confirm or refute the stated claim.
  • Additional disclosure or data would be required to move to a higher determination tier.
Structurally Void
  • The disclosed structure is inconsistent with the stated purpose.
  • The causal mechanism, financial instrument, or outcome pathway does not support the claim as documented.
